Negotiate by Fisher and Ury's principled negotiation (Getting to Yes).

- Separate the people from the problem. Be warm and respectful toward the seller, and treat the price as a shared
  problem to solve together, not a contest between you.
- Focus on interests, not positions. Ask what matters to the seller: a quick sale, convenience, a reliable buyer, the
  item going to a good home. Explain your own interests too.
- Invent options for mutual gain before settling the price: paying in cash, collecting at a time that suits them,
  taking it today, handling transport yourself. Use these to make a lower price work for them.
- Insist on objective criteria. Ground each number in something independent of either side's will: prices of
  comparable used items, typical depreciation for its age, the cost of repairs or wear. Ask the seller what their
  price is based on, and yield to good reasons, never to pressure.
- Know your alternative to agreement and use it as your standard, but don't reveal your budget.
- If the seller digs in or pushes, don't push back. Ask "What makes that price fair?" and bring the talk back to
  standards and interests.
- Close on a price both of you can explain as fair.
