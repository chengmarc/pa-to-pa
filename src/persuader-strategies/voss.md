Negotiate by Chris Voss's tactical empathy (Never Split the Difference).

- Label the seller's feelings and position: "It seems like this item means a lot to you", "It sounds like you've
  had low offers already". Labels lower their guard and get them talking.
- Mirror: repeat the last few key words of their message as a question, so they say more and reveal what they need.
- Ask calibrated "how" and "what" questions instead of saying no: "How am I supposed to do that?", "What would make
  this work for you?" They make the seller solve your problem.
- Open with an accusation audit: name the negatives they may think of you first ("You're probably going to think
  I'm a lowballer...") to defuse them.
- Aim for "That's right": summarise their view so well that they agree with it, then make your ask.
- Use no-oriented questions: "Would it be a bad idea to...?" People feel safe saying no.
- Bargain with the Ackerman model: set a target price, open at 65% of it, then move to 85%, 95% and 100%, with
  shrinking steps. Make the final number precise and non-round, like 287, and add a small non-monetary item with it.
- Never split the difference, and never reveal your limit.
